# Project 2 — Build for a Human Value

**Teams:** Same teams as Project 1\
**Worth:** 15% of your course grade  
**Assigned:** Thursday, July 23  
**Due:** Monday, July 27, before class  
**Join your team repo:** https://classroom.github.com/a/RkRB5iqO

## What are you making?

Build a small website or app that helps a **specific person** in a **specific situation**. Choose a human value such as privacy, accessibility, fairness, dignity, honesty, care, safety, autonomy, sustainability, or trust.

The app should not merely describe the value. Its features, language, defaults, and choices should actively support it.

> [!TIP]
> **Value ≠ topic.** “A recycling app” is a topic. “Helps renters without cars find the nearest drop-off, so sustainability is not gated by access to a car” names the value, user, and problem.

## What do you submit?

Submit a hosted app in `code_deliverable/`, your real AI interactions in `log_deliverable/history.md`, and one completed `project-report.md` containing your planning, first prompt, screenshots, red-team result, informal classmate test, and reflections. Put the public app link in the report.

The informal test belongs inside `project-report.md`; Project 2 does not require a separate `user-test.md`.

## Frame the project

Before building, answer five questions together: What value are you supporting? Who is the app for? What problem or decision are they facing? What will the app help them do? How will you know it is working?

Also name one tradeoff, such as supporting privacy even if collecting less data reduces personalization.

Your project may help someone act on a value, notice or avoid a harm, improve a harmful design, or use technology to defend against technology.

> [!TIP]
> You do not need to solve a huge social problem. Make one thoughtful, defensible improvement for one user.

# What to do in class

## Step 1 — Choose your value and user

Choose one value and one specific user—not “everyone.” Write: “We are building **[tool]** for **[user]** to help them **[action]** while supporting **[value]**.”

> [!TIP]
> “Students” is broad. “High school students who want to study without feeling guilty when they take breaks” gives you a person and situation to design for.

## Step 2 — Choose one main action

Decide on one small task the user can complete from beginning to end, such as comparing choices, checking a message, understanding a privacy risk, avoiding manipulation, or protecting personal information.

> [!TIP]
> One complete action is stronger than several unfinished features.

## Step 3 — Compare three ideas

Consider at least three approaches before choosing one. For each, ask how it supports the value, what could go wrong, and whether you can finish it by Monday. Record the ideas and your decision in `project-report.md`.

> [!TIP]
> Do not accept the AI’s first idea automatically. Ask for alternatives and make the decision as a team.

## Step 4 — Write your first prompt

Before asking AI to build, write a prompt that names the user, situation, value, main task, and one behavior or design choice to avoid. Paste the exact prompt into `project-report.md`.

> [!TIP]
> Instead of “Build a habit tracker,” try: “Build a habit tracker for a student recovering from burnout. Support consistency without shame, and do not use guilt, lost streaks, or threatening notifications.”

## Step 5 — Build the smallest working version

Create one complete experience that opens from a public link and lets a classmate finish the main task without your help. The value should appear in the design itself, not only in explanatory text.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Remove broken buttons, placeholders, and fake features. Finish the main flow before adding extras.

## Step 6 — Check the AI’s work

Test every button, link, instruction, claim, and number. Look for invented information, unnecessary tracking, excessive data collection, manipulative language, or defaults that conflict with your value. Fix or remove anything you cannot defend.

## Step 7 — Red-team the app

Test one realistic way the app could pressure, mislead, exclude, expose, or disadvantage someone. Record the risk, what happened, and what you changed in `project-report.md`.

> [!TIP]
> Find one believable harm and improve the design; you do not need to invent a disaster.

## Step 8 — Test with 1–2 classmates

Give each tester one realistic task, stay quiet, and watch for hesitation, confusion, wrong turns, pressure, or a value they interpret differently than you intended. Ask what they thought the tool was helping them do, what value it supported, and where they felt unsure. Make at least one change based on what you observe.

> [!TIP]
> Give the tester a task, not a tour. Confusion is useful evidence.

## Step 9 — Finish and submit

Open the public link in a private/incognito window, add it to the report, confirm the AI log is complete, finish the human-written ethical and individual reflections, and make sure every team member’s work is committed. Push the app, AI log, and report before the deadline in `deadline.json`, then complete the required Canvas submission.
